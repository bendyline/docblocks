# Implementation of the Coastal Resilience Programme

## Report of the Secretary-General

### I. Introduction

1. The present report is submitted in response to the request of the General Assembly that the Secretary-General report annually on the Coastal Resilience Programme. It covers the period from 1 January to 31 December 2025.

2. The Programme, established in 2019, supports 14 small island developing States in protecting coastal communities from sea-level rise and storm surge. It is coordinated by the Office for Coastal Resilience in Suva.

### II. Financing

3. Contributions to the CRF reached $48.6 million in 2025, an increase of 12 per cent over 2024. The Coastal Resilience Fund (CRF), a pooled trust fund, finances the Programme's field projects and is open to contributions from all Member States.

4. The annual pledging conference was held in Suva on March 14, 2025. At the conference, 5 delegations announced new multi-year commitments, and two private foundations made their first contributions.

### III. Activities

5. Coastal erosion in the participating States has accelerated since the 90's, and mapping that erosion remains the Programme's first priority. In 2025, aerial surveys covered 2,140 kilometres of coastline in Fiji, Samoa and Vanuatu.

6. A regional training program for 320 coastal engineers was completed in three phases. Of the participants, 46 per cent were women.

7. Early-warning sirens were installed in 38 villages. The new early-warning system has been a real game-changer for island communities.

8. Each fisherman and his family in the pilot villages received a storm-preparedness kit.

9. Several member states requested technical assistance to prepare national coastal adaptation plans. The Office expects to respond to all requests in 2026.

### IV. Challenges

10. Frankly, the results so far have been pretty disappointing. Some donors talk a big game at conferences and then fail to deliver, and it is high time they put their money where their mouth is.

### V. Recommendations

11. The Secretary-General recommends that the General Assembly encourage Member States to make multi-year contributions to the Fund and to share coastal data with the Office.
