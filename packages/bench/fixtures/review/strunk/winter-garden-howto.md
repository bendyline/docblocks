# Putting Your Garden Beds to Bed for Winter

A few hours of work in late autumn saves a great deal of work in spring. This guide covers clearing, feeding, mulching, and protecting your beds before the ground freezes.

## Clear the beds

Pull out spent annuals, diseased leaves and fallen fruit. Leave healthy seed heads standing, birds feed on them through the winter. The gardener, after the first hard frost, once the leaves have dropped, should cut perennial stems back to a hand's width above the soil.

## Feed the soil

Having cleared the beds, compost should be spread two inches deep over the soil. Worms will pull it down over the winter, and the beds will be ready to plant in April.

Beginners often do not pay attention to drainage. Water that sits on a bed for days rots roots faster than any frost.

## Mulch

Mulch protects roots from frost, keeps weeds down, and the soil stays moist. Use shredded leaves, straw, or bark, and spread it three inches deep once the soil has cooled. Your shears and loppers should be cleaned and oiled before you hang them up for the winter. Keep mulch a few inches away from tree trunks and the crowns of perennials.

## Protect tender plants

Wrap young shrubs in burlap and tie it loosely. Move pots of tender herbs against a south-facing wall or into an unheated shed.

## What to expect

Results will depend on a variety of factors. Beds you clear, feed, and mulch this way usually come through the winter with fewer weeds and looser soil.
