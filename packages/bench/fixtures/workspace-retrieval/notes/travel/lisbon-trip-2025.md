# Lisbon, 6 to 12 May 2025 (with Mina)

Flew out Tuesday evening, landed 9:10 am Wednesday. Six nights. Took the metro from the airport (red line, 40 minutes with one change, 1.85 per ticket plus 0.50 for the card).

## Where we stayed

The Alfama apartment was €96 a night, plus a €40 cleaning fee. Fourth floor with no lift, a tiny balcony facing the river and a washing machine that took 3 hours. Host was called Ines (not my cousin Ines, a different one). Check-in was a key box, code 4471. Hills are brutal with a suitcase; consider staying in Baixa next time.

## Day by day

### Wed 7 May
Slept 2 hours, walked up to the Miradouro de Santa Luzia, coffee and a pastel de nata (1.20 each). Dinner at a tiny place on Rua de São Miguel: grilled sardines, boiled potatoes, green salad. 32 euros for two with wine.

### Thu 8 May
Tram 28 in the morning before the crowds, 3.10 on the card. Stayed for two stops past Graça then walked. Afternoon at the Gulbenkian garden. Mina fell in love with the museum cafe.

### Fri 9 May
Day trip to Sintra by train from Rossio, 2.40 each way, 40 minutes. Bought timed tickets for the Pena Palace, 14 euros, 10:30 slot. Bus 434 between stops is the right call; the walk up is steep. Lunch at a cafe in the old town, travesseiros pastries for dessert. Came back exhausted.

### Sat 10 May
Belem: the tower, the monastery (queue was 45 minutes, skipped it) and the original custard tart shop, queue moved quickly. Rented bikes along the river for 2 hours, 8 euros each. Wind was strong coming back.

### Sun 11 May
Slow day. Flea market (Feira da Ladra) on Tuesday and Saturday only, so missed it. Ate bifana sandwiches in a hole in the wall near Time Out Market. Evening fado in a small bar, 25 euros including a drink, started at 9:30 and nobody was allowed to speak. Wonderful.

### Mon 12 May
Flight at 3:40 pm. Left the apartment at 11:15, taxi 18 euros.

## Food notes

## What I would change

1. Stay near a flat street.
2. Go to Sintra on a weekday in the morning, or skip Pena for Quinta da Regaleira.
3. Pack a lighter bag; cobblestones destroy wheels.
4. Learn "obrigado/obrigada" properly; Mina's accent got compliments.
5. Book the fado earlier, it sells out.

## Practical

- Weather: 18 to 24 C, one shower on the 10th
- Phone: eSIM, 5 GB for 12 euros
- Plug type: F (two prongs, round)
- Tipping: round up, 5 to 10 percent for sit-down meals
- Tram card: buy the rechargeable card on arrival, 0.50 euros

## Walking route to remember

From Alfama to the cathedral, then up Rua das Portas do Sol, straight to the miradouro. Downhill to Baixa takes 20 minutes. The 12 tram is a good slow way up to the castle for 3 euros. Many streets are stairs, so a map app that avoids steps helps.

## Journal bits

The first evening I felt completely out of it, jet lag mixed with wine, and fell asleep on the balcony. We woke up to the church bells at seven. Mina ordered "dois galões" in her best accent and got exactly what she asked for, an enormous latte, and was so pleased she took a photo of it. The old man at the corner shop learned our names by day three and held back the good bread. This is the part of travel I like best.

## Before we left

- Booked flights 11 weeks ahead in February; the cheaper fare had no checked bag, so we shared one 23 kg suitcase and a carry-on each
- Checked passports were valid for more than 6 months (they were)
- Left the spare key with Colm next door and asked him to bring the post in
- Told the bank about the trip, and withdrew 150 euros at the airport ATM rather than at a currency desk
- Downloaded the offline map of the city and the metro diagram
- Travel adapter and a short extension lead for the bedside table

## Health and safety

Pickpockets are active on tram 28 and in the Baixa crowds. I kept the wallet in the front pocket. Nothing lost. Mina's sunglasses fell through a gap in the viewpoint railing and were not recovered. Drank tap water throughout, no issues. A pharmacy near the apartment sold blister plasters for 4 euros after day two of cobblestones.

## Verdict

Eight out of ten. The food and the light were the best part. Too many hills for an old knee. Would go back in late September when it is quieter, and would pair it with a few nights on the coast at Cascais.
