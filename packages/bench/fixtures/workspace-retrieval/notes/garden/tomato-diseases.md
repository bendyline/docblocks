# Tomato diseases: what I saw and what to do

Written after the bad July of 2025 when I lost half the Brandywines.

## Early blight

Early blight shows as brown rings like a target on the lower leaves, then the leaf yellows and drops. Starts at the bottom and climbs.

- Remove affected leaves, bag them, do not compost
- Stop overhead watering, water at the base in the morning
- Mulch so rain does not splash soil up onto the leaves
- Copper spray every 7 to 10 days if it keeps spreading

## Late blight

Greasy, grey-green patches that go black in a day or two, white fuzz underneath in humid weather. This is the serious one. If the neighbours have it, you have it. Pull the whole plant, bag it, and do not grow potatoes in the same bed next year.

## Septoria leaf spot

Lots of tiny round spots with grey centres and dark edges. Looks bad but rarely kills the plant. Same treatment as early blight.

## Blossom end rot

Not a disease. Black leathery patch on the bottom of the fruit. Caused by uneven watering. Fix: deep watering twice a week, mulch.

## Fusarium and verticillium wilt

One side of the plant yellows and wilts, cut the stem and see brown inside. No cure. Next time buy varieties marked VFN on the label.

## Prevention checklist

- [ ] Rotate beds, never tomato in the same spot within 3 years
- [ ] Buy resistant varieties
- [ ] Spacing 24 inches for airflow
- [ ] Prune bottom 12 inches of foliage by mid-June
- [ ] Clean stakes with a bleach rinse each winter
- [ ] Morning watering only
