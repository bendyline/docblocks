# Garden log 2025

A running diary, newest at the bottom of each month. Written on the back step with a mug of tea, mostly.

## March

**3 Mar** Still frozen in the mornings. Cleaned pots, sharpened the pruners, ordered seeds (Sungold, Brandywine, a packet of Costata Romanesco zucchini, scarlet runner beans, Lacinato kale).

**14 Mar** Sowed tomatoes and peppers indoors. Peppers are slow; 10 days to germinate on the heat mat.

**22 Mar** Peas direct sown in bed D along the trellis. 2 rows, about 1 inch deep, 2 inches apart. Soaked overnight first.

**29 Mar** Soil test kit says pH 6.4. Added a bucket of compost per bed.

## April

**5 Apr** Raked off the winter leaf mulch. Found three crocus bulbs I had forgotten about.

**12 Apr** Radishes and lettuce seeded. Row cover over them because of the 2 C night forecast.

**19 Apr** Pruned the roses (Queen Elizabeth and the little yellow one by the gate) back to 18 inches. Cut out all the black-spotted wood.

**26 Apr** Peas are 4 inches tall. Mina thinks the rabbits will find them. They did, on the 28th. Chicken wire fence now, 2 feet high.

## May

**3 May** Hardening off tomato seedlings. Brought them in twice because of wind.

**10 May** Planted tomatoes in beds A and B. 16 plants total, a few too many for the space.

**17 May** Peppers into the pots on the patio. Basil planted under the Sungolds. Scarlet runner beans sown at the base of the teepee made of bamboo.

**24 May** Cabbage white butterflies are back. Mesh up over the kale.

**31 May** First radish harvest, 40 of them, too many. Gave a bag to the neighbours.

## June

**7 Jun** Mulched everything with straw, 3 bales. Cost 19 dollars.

**14 Jun** Peas finished, pulled the vines for compost. Replaced with bush beans.

**21 Jun** Longest day. Garlic scapes are curling. Cut them and made pesto, 3 jars, froze 2.

**28 Jun** First zucchini, 6 inches. By the end of the week there were seven. The Costata Romanesco is enormous.

## July

**4 Jul** Garlic harvested, 22 heads, a decent haul. Hung in the garage to cure for 3 weeks.

**8 Jul** First spots on the Brandywine leaves. Did not act fast enough (see the tomato disease note).

**12 Jul** Heat wave, 36 C for four days. Watered at 6 am and again at 8 pm. Shade cloth over the lettuce.

**19 Jul** Sungold tomatoes are ripening by the handful. Standing at the plant eating them warm is the best part of the year. Zucchini fritters for dinner three nights running.

**26 Jul** Cucumbers are bitter, probably the heat stress. Composted the lot.

## August

**2 Aug** Blueberries from the u-pick, 6 lb. Not from the garden but in the log because they went into the freezer.

**9 Aug** San Marzano tomatoes finally red. Made 9 quarts of sauce in one afternoon with Mina, the kitchen was 30 C.

**16 Aug** Second sowing of lettuce and a row of turnips.

**23 Aug** Peppers are doing well on the patio, 14 jalapenos picked. The bells are still green.

**30 Aug** Pulled the exhausted zucchini plant. Powdery mildew all over it.

## September

**6 Sep** Kale is thriving under the mesh. Picked a big bag.

**13 Sep** Planted spinach and a cover crop of crimson clover in bed B.

**20 Sep** Tomato total so far: weighed on the kitchen scale, 61 lb, of which 33 lb were Sungold and San Marzano.

**27 Sep** Dug up the dahlias early because of a cold snap forecast, cleaned the tubers and stored them in peat moss in the basement.

## October

**4 Oct** Autumn leaf pickup. Raked the neighbours' leaves too. The compost bin is full.

**11 Oct** Free wood chips arrived, a huge pile. Barrowed them onto the paths for three evenings.

**19 Oct** Planted garlic in bed C. 24 cloves from the best heads, 6 inches apart, 2 inches deep, mulched with straw.

**28 Oct** First frost hit on October 28, 2025, six days later than the average, and the last green tomatoes came in that afternoon. Wrapped about 15 lb of them in newspaper in a cardboard box in the garage; 9 ripened by Thanksgiving.

## November

**2 Nov** Cleared the beds, pulled the stakes, bleached them (dilute, 1 part to 9).

**9 Nov** Put the cover tarp on the compost. Drained the hose and the drip line.

**16 Nov** Planted 40 tulip bulbs in the front border, 6 inches deep, pointy end up. Added bone meal. Squirrels will get some.

**23 Nov** Kale still going, even after a skim of ice. It tastes sweeter after frost.

## What worked

- Straw mulch cut my watering in half
- Planting tomatoes deep
- Mesh over the brassicas from the start
- Drip line on a timer

## What did not

- Too many tomato plants, poor airflow, blight
- Cucumbers in full sun in the heat wave
- Zucchini overload (never plant 4 again, 2 is plenty)
- Not scouting for squash bugs weekly

## Plans for 2026

1. Fewer tomato plants (12 not 16) and 24 inch spacing
2. Rotate beds according to the layout note
3. Put a cold frame on bed D for winter greens
4. Try 2 rows of carrots, not 6 of radishes
5. Order seed in January, not March
6. Buy a rain barrel (60 gallon) and fit it to the shed gutter

## Odds and ends

- The cafe on Alder St gives away coffee grounds in a bucket by the door on Tuesdays and Fridays. Take two bags each visit and say thank you.
- Rain total for July was only 0.4 inches, so the drip line ran 25 minutes instead of 20 for the last two weeks.
- The rain gauge by the fence cracked in the November freeze; replace it with the plastic one, not glass.
- A hedgehog (or maybe a possum) has been visiting the compost bay at night. Leave the lid cracked on purpose.
- Neighbour Colm offered a trade, his rhubarb crowns for a bag of my compost. Do it in spring when the crowns can be divided.
- Seed packets live in the biscuit tin in the hall closet, sorted by month of sowing, not by plant.
