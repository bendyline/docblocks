# The Art of War (Sun Tzu): notes and quotes

Attributed to Sun Tzu, traditionally dated to the 5th century BC (the Spring and Autumn period), though scholars think it was compiled over time and finished by the Warring States era. Thirteen short chapters, easy to read in one sitting. I used the Samuel Griffith translation; Ames and Minford are also good.

## Chapter list

1. Laying Plans (assessment: Way, Heaven, Earth, Commander, Method)
2. Waging War
3. Attack by Stratagem
4. Tactical Dispositions
5. Energy
6. Weak Points and Strong
7. Maneuvering
8. Variation in Tactics
9. The Army on the March
10. Terrain
11. The Nine Situations
12. Attack by Fire
13. The Use of Spies

## Core ideas in my words

- The best victory is the one that never requires a battle. Breaking the enemy's plans or alliances comes first; besieging cities is the worst option because it is slow and costly.
- Know yourself and know your opponent. A commander who knows neither will lose every fight, one who knows only himself will win about half.
- Speed matters: no country has ever profited from a long war.
- Appear weak when strong, and strong when weak. Deception is the foundation of everything.
- Water has no constant shape; an army should take its form from the ground and the enemy.
- Spies are the cheapest investment: a day's espionage costs less than a campaign. He lists five types, including doomed spies who carry false information.

## Short quotes

- "All warfare is based on deception."
- "Supreme excellence consists in breaking the enemy's resistance without fighting."

## How people actually use it

Business books quote it endlessly. I think the better reading is about restraint and preparation: most of the book is about what to do before conflict, and why to avoid it.
