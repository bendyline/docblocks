# Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus (Mary Shelley)

Read the 1831 text. Written when Shelley was 18 to 19, begun in the summer of 1816 at Lake Geneva during the "year without a summer" after the Tambora eruption. Lord Byron proposed a ghost story contest; Mary's idea became the novel. First published anonymously in 1818.

## Frame structure

Nested narration, like Russian dolls: Captain Robert Walton writes letters to his sister from an Arctic expedition. He rescues Victor Frankenstein, who tells his story, inside which the creature tells his own. All of it reaches us through Walton's letters.

## Summary by section

**Letters 1-4.** Walton seeks the North Pole. His ship is trapped in ice; Victor is found on a sled.

**Volume I, chapters 1-7.** Victor's happy childhood in Geneva, with his adopted cousin Elizabeth. At university in Ingolstadt he studies natural philosophy and chemistry, becomes obsessed with creating life, and builds a being from assembled parts. In chapter 5, the creature opens its "dull yellow eye" and Victor flees in horror. He abandons it. Later his young brother William is murdered, and Justine, the family servant, is wrongly executed.

**Volume II, chapters 1-9.** Victor meets the creature on a glacier near Mont Blanc. The creature narrates: taught himself language by secretly watching the De Lacey family, read Paradise Lost, Plutarch, and Goethe's Werther, was driven away by violence when he revealed himself. He asks Victor to make a female companion.

**Volume III, chapters 1-7.** Victor starts, then destroys, the second creature on a remote Orkney island. The creature vows revenge: "I shall be with you on your wedding-night." He kills Victor's friend Clerval, then Elizabeth. Victor chases the creature north and dies aboard Walton's ship. The creature mourns and vanishes into the ice.

## Themes

- Responsibility of the creator: the monster is not born evil, he is made so by neglect. Victor never gives him a name.
- Dangerous knowledge and ambition, as in the Prometheus myth of the subtitle.
- Isolation and the need for companionship.

## Note

Pop culture got a lot wrong: the creature is articulate and well read, there is no Igor, no bolts in the neck, and no lightning scene in the book.
