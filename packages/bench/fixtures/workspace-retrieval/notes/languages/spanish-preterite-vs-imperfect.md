# Spanish: preterite vs imperfect

The two simple past tenses. English uses one form ("I ate") for both ideas, so this is the hardest part of Spanish grammar for me. Compare with my separate ser vs estar notes, which is the other classic stumbling block. Tutor session notes, week 11.

## The one-sentence rule

The imperfect paints the scenery of a story; the preterite moves the plot forward.

"Llovía y hacía frío cuando llegué a casa." (It was raining and it was cold [scenery, imperfect] when I arrived home [event, preterite].)

## Preterite (pretérito indefinido): finished actions

Use it for:

1. A single completed action: "Ayer compré un libro."
2. A sequence of events in order: "Me levanté, me duché y salí."
3. An action with stated start or end, or a stated duration: "Viví en Madrid durante tres años."
4. A change of state or reaction at a moment: "Tuve miedo cuando oí el ruido." (I got scared.)

Trigger words: ayer, anoche, el lunes pasado, de repente, una vez, dos veces, en 2019, durante.

### Regular endings

| | -ar (hablar) | -er/-ir (comer, vivir) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| yo | hablé | comí |
| tú | hablaste | comiste |
| él/ella | habló | comió |
| nosotros | hablamos | comimos |
| vosotros | hablasteis | comisteis |
| ellos | hablaron | comieron |

Accent marks on the yo and él forms matter: "hablo" (I speak) vs "habló" (he spoke).

### Irregular preterites

The irregular ones share one set of endings with no accents: -e, -iste, -o, -imos, -isteis, -ieron (-eron after j).

- tener becomes tuv- (tuve, tuviste, tuvo, tuvimos, tuvieron)
- estar becomes estuv-, andar becomes anduv-
- poder: pud-; poner: pus-; saber: sup-; querer: quis-; venir: vin-
- hacer: hic- (hizo with a z in the third person singular)
- decir: dij- (dijeron, not dijieron); traer: traj-; conducir: conduj-
- ser and ir are identical in the preterite: fui, fuiste, fue, fuimos, fuisteis, fueron. Context decides ("Fue un gran día" vs "Fue al mercado").
- dar: di, diste, dio, dimos, disteis, dieron (uses -er/-ir endings, no accents on di and dio).

Stem-changers in -ir verbs change only in the third person: dormir gives durmió and durmieron; pedir gives pidió and pidieron; sentir gives sintió.

Spelling changes in yo for -car, -gar, -zar: busqué, llegué, empecé. And -er/-ir verbs with a vowel before the ending turn the i to y: leyó, leyeron, cayó, construyó.

## Imperfect (pretérito imperfecto): ongoing or habitual past

Use it for:

1. Background description and setting: "La casa era grande y tenía un jardín."
2. Habitual or repeated past action ("used to", "would"): "Cada verano íbamos a la playa."
3. Ongoing action with no defined endpoint ("was doing"): "Mientras estudiaba, sonó el teléfono."
4. Age, time and weather in the past: "Tenía diez años." "Eran las tres."
5. Mental or emotional states in the past: "Quería ayudarte." "Pensaba que era fácil."

Trigger words: siempre, a menudo, todos los días, cada semana, mientras, de niño, generalmente, normalmente.

### Endings and irregulars

-ar: -aba, -abas, -aba, -ábamos, -abais, -aban. -er/-ir: -ía, -ías, -ía, -íamos, -íais, -ían.

Only three verbs are irregular, which is a relief:

- ser: era, eras, era, éramos, erais, eran
- ir: iba, ibas, iba, íbamos, ibais, iban
- ver: veía, veías, veía, veíamos, veíais, veían

## Using both in one sentence

Interrupted action: the imperfect gives the ongoing action, the preterite is the interruption. "Estaba cenando cuando llegó mi hermano."

Two simultaneous ongoing actions use two imperfects joined by mientras: "Yo leía mientras ella cocinaba."

A sequence of completed events uses preterites only: "Entró, miró alrededor y se fue."

## Verbs that change meaning

| Verb | Imperfect | Preterite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| saber | knew (a fact) | found out |
| conocer | knew (a person) | met |
| querer | wanted | tried (affirmative); refused (negative) |
| poder | was able / could (in general) | managed to |
| tener que | had to (maybe did, maybe not) | had to and did |

"No pude abrir la puerta" means I tried and failed; "No podía abrir la puerta" only says the situation, I was unable.

## Practice paragraph I wrote

"Cuando era niña, vivía en un pueblo pequeño. Todos los sábados mi abuela me llevaba al mercado. Un sábado, mientras caminábamos, vi a un perro perdido. Lo seguí, y de repente me di cuenta de que no veía a mi abuela. Tuve mucho miedo, pero ella me encontró cinco minutos después."

Corrections my tutor made: "caminabamos" needs the accent (caminábamos), and "Lo seguí" is right because it is a completed action.

## Mnemonic

Preterite = a photograph of a moment; imperfect = a video of the background. Another trick: ask whether I could add "used to" or "was ...-ing" in English; if yes, imperfect.

## Extra: contrasts the tutor drew on the whiteboard

Compare "Fui a la tienda" (I went to the shop, a completed trip) with "Iba a la tienda" (I was going to the shop, with something happening along the way, or an habitual trip when I was young). The first closes the action; the second leaves it open. The same distinction explains why a story told only in the preterite feels like a news report: events pile up without any picture of the world around them. A story told only in the imperfect feels like a dream, with a world but nothing happening.

Another useful test is translating into English questions. "¿Qué hacías?" asks what you were doing at some background moment; "¿Qué hiciste?" asks what you did, as a completed thing. Likewise "¿Dónde vivías de niño?" (where did you live as a child) takes the imperfect because the residence was an ongoing state, whereas "¿Dónde viviste en 2015?" with a bounded year can take the preterite.

Weather and descriptions deserve one more word: "Hizo mucho calor ayer" is possible when the whole day is treated as a single bounded event, but "Hacía mucho calor cuando salimos" is the normal choice when the heat is the setting. Native speakers sometimes flip the choice on purpose for effect, and my tutor says that is the point of the whole system: the speaker chooses how to frame the action, instead of the grammar choosing for them.

Common errors on my corrected homework: using the imperfect with "ayer" plus a completed event; forgetting the accent on first person preterite of -er verbs; and using "fue" for both "he went" and "it was" without noticing that a sentence needed the other one.
