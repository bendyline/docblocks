# Sourdough: why my loaves come out dense

Notes after three bad bakes in a row (14, 19 and 26 October 2025). The starter was fine each time, so it is a process problem.

## Symptoms and causes

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tight crumb, gummy stripe near the bottom | Underproofed | Longer bulk, wait for 50 percent growth |
| Flat, spread-out loaf, big holes under the crust | Overproofed | Shorten the final proof, bake straight from the fridge |
| Pale crust, soft after cooling | Not enough steam or oven not hot | Preheat the Dutch oven for a full 45 minutes at 245 C |
| Dense but with one giant tunnel | Shaping too loose | Tighter surface tension, use a bench scraper |

## What I decided

Dense, gummy crumb nearly always meant the shaping was fine but bulk fermentation ended too early. My kitchen drops to about 18 C in the evening so the clock-based recipe (4 hours) is wrong for autumn. Go by the dough, not the clock.

## Checks before shaping

- Jiggly, domed top with bubbles at the edge of the bowl
- Dough has grown by roughly half, not doubled
- Poke test: dent springs back slowly, not instantly and not never
- Warm spot for bulk: oven with only the light on, about 26 C

## Recipe I am returning to

- 450 g bread flour, 50 g whole wheat
- 365 g water (73 percent hydration)
- 100 g active starter
- 10 g salt
- Bulk 5 to 6 hours in the light-on oven, 3 sets of stretch and folds in the first 90 minutes
- Overnight in the fridge for 14 hours, bake 20 minutes lid on, 22 minutes lid off
